## Snapshot of findings

**Interpretation:** The report describes Jio as stronger on scale, revenue growth, net margin and headline net debt; Airtel as stronger on ARPU, operating margin, profit momentum and operating history. The report suggests Jio traded at a premium on EV/EBITDA, subject to adjustments for debt-like spectrum obligations and minority interests.

## Research methodology

1. **Subscriber scale:** Compare total subscribers, customer additions and wireless market share.
2. **ARPU and monetisation:** Review trends and differences in subscriber mix and metric definitions.
3. **Profitability:** Compare revenue, EBITDA, margins, attributable net profit and growth rates.
4. **Balance sheet and cash flow:** Assess debt and leverage; flag missing Jio capex and free cash flow.
5. **Relative valuation:** Review P/E and EV/EBITDA, accounting for differences in reporting basis.
6. **Industry and risk analysis:** Consider tariffs, 5G, broadband, competition, spectrum liabilities and capital intensity.

## Important data reconciliations

The research report identifies several workbook figures requiring reconciliation:

- Jio Q2 revenue: workbook ₹47,485 crore versus ₹40,475 crore revenue from operations used in the report.
- Jio EBITDA margin: workbook 46.1% versus 54.1% on the report's revenue basis.
- Airtel profit: ₹10,710.4 crore before minority interests versus ₹8,887.5 crore attributable to shareholders.
- ARPU: older workbook figures of ₹215.6 (Jio) and ₹263.9 (Airtel) versus Q2 figures of ₹218.8 and ₹271.
- P/E: workbook ratios differ from the report's recalculation on annualised Q2 attributable earnings.
- Airtel's India mobile ARPU and Jio's reported ARPU are **not perfectly comparable**.

These are findings and adjustments *within the supplied report*, not independent confirmation from primary sources.

## Research conclusion

The report frames **Jio as a scale and growth opportunity** and **Airtel as an execution and cash-flow visibility story**. Jio's growth and balance-sheet position appear favorable on the reported basis, while Airtel has stronger ARPU and operating margins. Valuation comparisons remain sensitive to how leases, deferred spectrum liabilities and minority interests are handled.

## Limitations and next steps

- Validate the November 2026 market snapshot and Q2 FY27 figures against official company, exchange and TRAI records.
- Confirm Jio's revenue definition, deferred spectrum liabilities, and net debt basis.
- Extend the study to multiple years of financial results and comparable subscriber definitions.
- Add Jio capex, free cash flow, and cash conversion metrics.
- Include Vodafone Idea as a third telecom peer and test valuation sensitivities.
- Avoid treating one annualised quarter as a reliable full-year forecast.
